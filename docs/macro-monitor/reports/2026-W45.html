Weekly macro briefing

Hot economy confirmed: payrolls and ISM firm, inflation pipeline hot, BoE hikes, while bonds keep rallying

Executive summary

This week's data met last week's tests for a hot-economy regime. October payrolls rose 165k after 60k, unemployment fell to 4.1% and average hourly earnings rose 0.37% m/m. Initial claims fell to 189,000 and September JOLTS openings rose 333k to 7.52 million. ISM Services held at 54.5 and the unconfirmed ISM Manufacturing print was 55.5, with prices paid at 73.0 and 75.2. That lines up labour, growth and the early inflation pipeline in one direction, on top of September core PCE at 3.1% y/y. The BoE hiked 25bp to 4.0%, the first hike among the three central banks we track, and an ECB hike in December remains in play. Markets disagree: in the week to 4 Nov the 10y Treasury fell 15bp to 4.90% and 10y breakevens fell 12bp, while HY spreads widened 18bp. October CPI next week is the main test.

What the data is saying

Inflation and policy

No new US inflation data came out this week, so the latest chain is still September: PPI up 1.3% m/m with core at 0.7%, core CPI 0.3% m/m against 0.2% expected, and core PCE 0.3% m/m and 3.1% y/y, 1.1pp above target and rising. The October surveys say the pipeline has not cooled. ISM Services prices were 73.0 and ISM Manufacturing prices paid 75.2 (unconfirmed, single publisher), both signalling rising input costs. The Fed held at 3.75-4.00% on 2026-10-28 with two dissents for a hike, so the live choice at the next meeting is a hold or a hike. The BoE hiked 25bp to 4.0% from 3.75%, voting 8-1 with Mann for a larger hike; the vote split is unconfirmed by a second source. Guidance is conditional on energy and second-round effects. The ECB held at 2.50%, but euro area HICP at 3.75% y/y keeps a December hike in play.

Labour

Last week we said payrolls near zero, unemployment above 4.2% or falling openings would mark a labour turn. The opposite happened. October payrolls rose 165k after 60k, and unemployment fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.37% m/m, faster than the prior 0.27%, though y/y wages eased to 3.1%. September JOLTS openings rose to 7.52 million and the quits rate to 2.1% from 1.9%, which tends to lead firmer pay. Initial claims fell 6k to 189,000, the 4-week average is 194,750 and continuing claims are 1.718 million, all well below the 1.8 million continuing-claims level that would mark a turn. ADP added 86k. Consensus figures were not found for these prints, so we do not score surprises. In the UK, unemployment is 4.7% with regular pay at 3.7%, data for the 3 months to August that predate the BoE hike.

Growth, housing and sentiment

Growth stays strong into the fourth quarter. ISM Services was 54.5 after 54.9, with new orders down to 55.9 from 59.8 but employment up to 51.4. ISM Manufacturing at 55.5 with new orders at 55.5 points the same way, but it rests on one publisher and is unconfirmed. These follow Q3 advance GDP at 3.4% SAAR. The September trade deficit was 105.0bn, little changed from 105.7bn, so it adds no new swing to Q3 net exports. The October CARTS retail nowcast could not be found or confirmed and remains unanalysed. Housing had no new releases; the latest data show September starts down 5.2% to 1.22 million and permits steady near 1.40 million, so housing remains the weak link. Sentiment also had no new prints: UMich at 48.0 with 5y inflation expectations at 3.5%, and the Conference Board at 91.7 (unconfirmed).

Financial conditions

Market data run to 4 Nov, so they include ISM Services but not payrolls or the BoE decision. In the week to 4 Nov the 10y Treasury fell 15bp to 4.90% and the 2y 9bp to 4.58%, flattening 10y-2y by 6bp to 0.32%. The 10y-3m spread narrowed 18bp to 0.69% as the 3m bill rose 3bp. 10y breakevens fell 12bp to 2.26% and 5y5y 5bp to 2.22%, so the rally came mostly from lower inflation pricing, with the 10y real yield down only 3bp. HY spreads widened 18bp to 3.32% and IG 5bp to 0.91%; VIX was steady at 16.6. The NFCI at -0.459 is still loose but tightened for a fifth week, and the Q4 SLOOS shows a net 1.7% of banks tightening C&I standards, up from zero. The broad dollar index lags to 30 Oct and is not quoted. Bonds and credit are pricing a slowdown the macro data do not yet show.

Regime watch

The hot-economy regime is now aligned across labour, growth, the inflation pipeline and policy. It firms further if October CPI shows core at 0.3% m/m or more and PPI stays hot, which would make a Fed hike live. It weakens if core CPI drops to 0.2% m/m or below, the 4-week claims average rises well above 194,750, or ISM new orders fall into contraction. Markets are the open question: if Treasury yields and breakevens keep falling while HY spreads widen and the NFCI moves toward zero, financial conditions would be leading the economy lower, the first sign of a turn the data have not yet confirmed.
