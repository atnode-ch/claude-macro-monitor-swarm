Weekly macro briefing

Inflation re-accelerating into firm nominal demand; labour steady, conditions still loose

Executive summary

Week 42 was an inflation week. September CPI ran hot on core, 0.34% m/m against 0.22% consensus, with core y/y at 2.56% and headline at 3.74%. PPI was hotter still: headline +1.3% m/m and core +0.7% m/m, 5.0% y/y. Euro area HICP rose to 3.75% y/y, above the 3.6% expectation. Three inflation prints in two regions point the same way, which is signal, not noise. Activity did not soften to offset it: retail sales rose 0.77% m/m and industrial production 0.66% m/m. Labour stayed low-hire, low-fire, with initial claims at 199,000. Financial conditions remain loose, NFCI at -0.499, though it has tightened slightly two weeks running. The 10y yield fell 18bp to 5.10% over 5 days despite the hot prints, while the 2y was flat at 4.77%. The mix argues against near-term easing. Sentiment has no logged stance and its latest print is from August.

What the data is saying

Inflation and policy

The ISM to PPI to CPI chain is lining up. September PPI headline rose 1.3% m/m, about 0.7pp above consensus (unconfirmed), and y/y reached 6.3%. Energy drove much of that, but core PPI also jumped to +0.7% m/m and 5.0% y/y. CPI followed in the same month: core +0.34% m/m against 0.22% expected, core y/y 2.56% against 2.4%, headline y/y 3.74% against 3.6%. Since PPI leads CPI by 1 to 3 months, the October and November CPI prints carry upside risk. The latest core PCE in the log is August at 3.0% y/y, so the Fed's preferred gauge has not yet absorbed September. The FOMC upper bound sits at 4.0% after the September decision. In Europe, HICP rose to 3.75% y/y from 3.23%, a 3-year high led by energy, with core steady near 2.5%. Two regions with energy-led headline acceleration and firmer core in the US is the alignment to watch. Market-implied inflation is calm: 5y5y breakevens held at 2.33% and 10y breakevens at 2.35%, both unchanged on the week.

Labour

Labour is not turning. Initial claims were 199,000 against 200,000 consensus (unconfirmed), the 4-week average 198,500, and continuing claims flat at 1.71M. The other legs of the chain are older baseline prints: JOLTS openings fell 256k to 7.08M in August, quits rate 1.9%, ADP added 90k in September and NFP added 29k with unemployment up 0.1pt to 4.2%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.13% m/m and 3.0% y/y, so wages are not feeding the CPI acceleration. The pattern is slow hiring without layoffs. A turn would need claims rising alongside weaker openings and payrolls; only the claims leg updated this week and it was flat. The view changes if the 4-week average climbs above 215,000 or continuing claims pass 1.8M.

Growth, housing and sentiment

Nominal demand is firm. Retail sales rose 0.77% m/m against 0.3% consensus after +1.29% in August, and 6.29% y/y. Gas stations contributed much of the beat on price, so real consumption is softer than the headline, and ex-gas was reported near +0.2% (unconfirmed). Ex-auto sales rose 0.72%. Industrial production added 0.66% m/m, 2.25% y/y, with capacity utilisation up to 76.85%. Both are hard-data support for Q3 GDP after Q2 at 2.2% SAAR. August core capex orders rose 1.56%. Housing is weak at a low level: existing home sales rose 1.5% to 3.99M SAAR, slightly above the 3.95M consensus, but this is a lagging closings count. The leading series, permits, fell 2.1% to 1.40M in August, and starts fell 2.6%. Sentiment has no stance; the latest UMich reading is August at 51.7, down 3.5 points, which is stale for this synthesis.

Financial conditions

Conditions are loose but drifting tighter at the margin. NFCI rose 0.008 to -0.499, a second small tightening week, still well below zero. HY OAS widened 8bp to 3.23% and IG OAS 2bp to 0.84%, both from tight levels. The curve flattened: 10y-2y narrowed 12bp to 0.35% and 10y-3m 16bp to 0.83%, as the 10y fell 18bp to 5.10% and the 2y held at 4.77%. The 10y real yield fell 14bp to 2.78%, so the long-end rally was real rates, not breakevens. VIX rose 1.1 points to 16.46. Long yields falling into hot inflation prints is a mismatch with the macro data; if it reflects growth worries, it should show up in wider spreads and weaker activity data first. Q3 SLOOS showed net C&I tightening at 0%, so bank credit is not a constraint.

Regime watch

The firming-inflation regime is confirmed if core CPI m/m stays above 0.3% next month, core PCE for September rises from 3.0% y/y, and core PPI does not retrace. It is denied if energy reverses and core PPI drops back near 0.3% m/m. A shift toward slowdown would need claims above a 215,000 4-week average, falling permits again, and HY spreads widening well beyond this week's 8bp, together rather than alone.
