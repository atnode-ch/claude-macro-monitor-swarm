What the data is saying

Inflation and policy

No new US inflation print this week. The September set remains hot: core CPI 0.3% m/m against 0.2% consensus, core y/y 2.6%, headline 3.7%. PPI ran ahead of it, headline +1.3% m/m and core +0.7% m/m, 5.0% y/y. PPI leads CPI by 1 to 3 months, so the pipeline still points up. The last logged core PCE is 3.0% y/y for August; September PCE next week is the first test of PPI pass-through into the Fed's measure. The FOMC hiked 25bp in September to a 3.75% to 4.00% range. The live question for next week is whether it hikes again, with hot inflation on one side and weak payrolls on the other. Rising consumer inflation expectations strengthen the case for another move. In the euro area, HICP at 3.75% against 3.6% expected keeps the ECB from easing. UK pay at 3.7% keeps BoE hike risk live, with Bank Rate at 3.75%.

Labour

The US labour market is low-hire, low-fire. Initial claims fell 6,000 to 194,000, the 4-week average eased to 197,750 and continuing claims are 1.728 million, below the 1.8 million line that would signal slack. September payrolls were weak at +29,000 with unemployment at 4.2%, and ADP showed +90,000, but layoffs are not rising. JOLTS openings fell 256,000 in August. That is a slow-hiring market, not a turning one; a turn needs claims to rise alongside falling openings. In the UK, unemployment was 4.7%, below the 4.9% consensus, and vacancies fell to 691,000. Demand for labour is cooling there without unemployment rising.

Growth, housing and sentiment

Activity data is firm. Retail sales rose 0.8% m/m in September, though gasoline drove much of it, and industrial production rose 0.7% with capacity use at 76.85. Both support Q3 GDP, due next week after 2.2% in Q2. Housing is the weak spot. Starts fell 5.2% to 1.22 million SAAR after a 5.9% fall in August, so residential investment will be a drag in Q3. Permits held near 1.40 million, down 0.2%, which argues for stabilisation rather than a further slide. Existing home sales rose 1.5% to 3.99 million, slightly above consensus. October UMich sentiment was 48.0, flat from 48.1, so it adds no new weakness. The signal in the survey is inflation: year-ahead expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6% and the 5 to 10 year measure to 3.5% from 3.4%.

Financial conditions

Week to 23 Oct. Conditions are loose and drifting tighter at a slow pace. NFCI rose 0.006 to -0.496, a third small tightening week. HY OAS widened 7bp to 3.38% and IG OAS 6bp to 0.91%, while VIX eased 0.5 points to 19.45. The bond market carried the move: the 10y yield fell 9bp to 5.00%, the 2y 5bp to 4.69% and the 10y real yield 8bp to 2.66%. 5y5y breakevens fell 13bp to 2.20%. The 10y-3m curve narrowed 5bp to 0.78% and 10y-2y 4bp to 0.31%. Falling real yields and long-run breakevens read as growth worry in rates, but credit and volatility do not confirm it this week. Market inflation pricing is falling while household expectations rise, a gap the FOMC will weigh.

Regime watch

The firming-inflation regime is confirmed if September core PCE rises above 3.0% y/y, the FOMC keeps another hike on the table and consumer inflation expectations keep rising. A shift toward slowdown needs the rates signal to reach credit and hard data: Q3 GDP below the 2.2% Q2 pace, a 4-week claims average above 215,000, and HY spreads widening well beyond this week's 7bp. If that happens while core PCE runs hot, that is the stagflation mix the Fed cannot ease into.
