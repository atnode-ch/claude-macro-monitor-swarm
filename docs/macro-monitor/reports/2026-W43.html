Inflation firm and hard data solid, but spreads, housing and sentiment start to soften

Executive summary

Week 43 added no new inflation data, so last week's hot September prints still set the tone: core CPI 0.34% m/m, core PPI 5.0% y/y, euro area HICP 3.75%. Hard US activity data stays firm, with initial claims at 194,000 and a 4-week average of 197,750. The new information came from the soft side. UMich sentiment fell 3.6 points to 48.1, housing starts fell 5.2% for a second straight drop, and HY OAS widened 17bp to 3.40% with VIX up 2.6 points to 19.03. The 10y yield fell 6bp to 5.13% and 5y5y breakevens fell 12bp to 2.19%, so markets are pricing slower growth, not more inflation. Three domains (markets, housing, sentiment) now lean the same way, which is worth tracking, but claims, permits and retail do not confirm it. The regime holds for now. Next week carries the FOMC, the ECB, September PCE and Q3 GDP.

What the data is saying

Inflation and policy

No new US inflation print this week. The September set remains hot: core CPI 0.34% m/m against 0.22% consensus, core y/y 2.56%, headline 3.74%. PPI ran ahead of it, headline +1.3% m/m and core +0.7% m/m, 5.0% y/y. PPI leads CPI by 1 to 3 months, so the pipeline still points up. The last logged core PCE is 3.0% y/y for August; September PCE next week is the first test of whether the PPI pass-through shows up in the Fed's measure. The FOMC target range is 3.75% to 4.00% after a 25bp move in September. With core inflation broadening and payroll growth weak, the FOMC faces a dual-mandate tension, and the hot inflation side argues against easing. In the euro area, HICP at 3.75% against 3.6% expected keeps the ECB on hold at best. UK pay at 3.7% keeps BoE hike risk live, with Bank Rate at 3.75%.

Labour

The US labour market is low-hire, low-fire. Initial claims fell 6,000 to 194,000, the 4-week average eased to 197,750 and continuing claims are 1.728 million, below the 1.8 million line that would signal slack. September payrolls were weak at +29,000 with unemployment at 4.2%, and ADP showed +90,000, but layoffs are not rising. JOLTS openings fell 256,000 in August. That is a slow-hiring market, not a turning one; a turn needs claims to rise alongside falling openings. In the UK, unemployment fell to 4.7% from 4.9% while vacancies hit 691,000, a 5-year low. Demand for labour is cooling there without unemployment rising.

Growth, housing and sentiment

Activity data is firm. Retail sales rose 0.77% m/m in September, though gasoline drove much of it, and industrial production rose 0.66% with capacity use at 76.85. Both support Q3 GDP, which is due next week after 2.2% in Q2. Housing is the weak spot. Starts fell 5.2% to 1.22 million SAAR after a 5.9% fall in August, so residential investment will be a drag in Q3. Permits held near 1.40 million, down only 0.2%, which argues for stabilisation rather than a further slide. Existing home sales rose 1.5% to 3.99 million, slightly above consensus. UMich sentiment fell to 48.1 from 51.7. Sentiment and spending often diverge, so this needs hard-data confirmation before it counts.

Financial conditions

Conditions are loose but tightening at the margin, and this week the move was broader. NFCI rose 0.006 to -0.496, a third small tightening week. HY OAS widened 17bp to 3.40%, more than double last week's widening, and IG OAS 5bp to 0.89%. VIX rose to 19.03. At the same time the 10y yield fell 6bp to 5.13%, the 10y real yield fell 8bp to 2.76% and 5y5y breakevens fell 12bp to 2.19%. The 10y-3m curve narrowed 8bp to 0.74% and 10y-2y 2bp to 0.31%. Wider spreads, a higher VIX and lower long-run inflation pricing together read as growth worry, not inflation fear. That conflicts with the hot inflation prints, and it is the first market signal that lines up with weaker housing and sentiment.

Regime watch

The firming-inflation regime is confirmed if September core PCE rises above 3.0% y/y and the FOMC signals no easing. A shift toward slowdown needs the soft signals to reach hard data: Q3 GDP below the 2.2% Q2 pace, a 4-week claims average above 215,000, and HY spreads widening again next week. If spreads widen while core PCE runs hot, that is the stagflation mix the Fed cannot ease into. A reversal in HY spreads and a VIX back below 17 would deny it.
